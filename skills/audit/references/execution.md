# Execution and continuity — contract 0.4

## Framing and exposure

Give experts a neutral brief: objective/target identity, requirements/sources,
frozen obligations, selected profile/common contract, tools, permissions and limits.
Exclude parent history, hypotheses, other experts' results and adjudications.
Keep those in the orchestrator-only checkpoint with mission/context IDs, selection
evidence, lifecycle, outstanding checks and resource ownership. Retain it in
orchestration state; if saving was requested, declare location/access limits and
never direct initial experts to it. Fresh contexts do not isolate shared files.

Identify previous-report paths from the request/project report location without
reading conclusions, including while freezing the grid. Exclude report-only paths
from initial reads/searches and instruct experts likewise until comparison. Keep
required authority sources accessible even if they contain old conclusions.
Disclose prior, accidental or unavoidable exposure; do not claim total independence.

## Selection

Freeze one policy relative to the orchestrator:

- `same-model` (compatibility default): exact current model, selected explicitly or
  through documented parent-model inheritance.
- `different-model`: a distinct model; provider may be the same.
- `different-provider`: a distinct runtime-identified provider, not inferred from
  model branding. Experts may share that provider unless the mission says otherwise.

Discover actual capability/model/provider IDs. Use native or application delegation
that supports selection and fresh contexts without parent history. Record requested
selection, supported selector/inheritance guarantee and its source, and actual
exposed identity or `not exposed` separately. An explicit supported selector or
documented guarantee can suffice; a request, vague default or ambiguous alias cannot
be claimed as observed identity. Stop unsupported delegation/context separation;
no simulated specialists or silent substitution. Policy changes need explicit
authorization. Parallelize within capacity; wait only on returned live IDs.

## Checks and resources

Before costly checks, verify relevant database identity/schema, migrations,
sufficient fixtures, ports, installed dependencies, browser access and target URL.
Missing setup is a limit, not product failure. The orchestrator owns shared checks
and distributes evidence locators; assign targeted checks to authorized experts
without duplication or resource conflicts.

In the brief distinguish read-only checks (including external contacts), disposable
local artifacts/locations, explicitly authorized disposable fixture writes/migrations,
and project/existing-service modifications requiring separate permission. An audit
authorizes no installation, migration or restart of existing environments. Report
unmet permissions and continue independent checks. Every temporary resource needs
owner, location, purpose and final disposition: deleted, or retained with reason.
Coordinate cleanup on completion/suspension with its owner; preserve resources
needed by a pending continuation.

## Mission lifecycle

Checkpoint each mission's live task/context IDs, role, original brief, target,
provider/model and selection evidence, last status and result/evidence locators:

- `running`: active;
- `suspended`: quota/temporary limit; pending, not final missing coverage;
- `completed`: received result linked to original mission;
- `terminal failure`: runtime confirms no continuation or pending resume; affected
  obligations unverified unless other evidence establishes their state.

Quota suspends the original mission. Inspect exposed status/resume capabilities
before declaring terminal failure. Preserve task, context, model/provider and IDs;
attach resumed results to the original mission. A linked runtime continuation must
be traced; a fresh mission is not silently called a resume. Never duplicate work
while old executions or scheduled resumes could still run.

Use runtime notifications/resumption, not an added watcher/poll loop. Promise no
automatic recovery without confirmed capability. If continuation cannot be confirmed,
retain the checkpoint and report suspension, next check/resume route and incomplete
objective. A provisional handoff may describe established evidence, not finalize
coverage. Exceptional replacement needs explicit authorization and verified cessation
of all old executions/resumes, even after terminal failure; record reason, old/new
IDs and context/evidence discontinuity.
